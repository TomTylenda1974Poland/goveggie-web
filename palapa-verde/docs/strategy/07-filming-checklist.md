# Filming checklist: one efficient kitchen session

## Output and boundaries

Capture several weeks of real footage for v01–v11, c02 and pins 1–2 in one kitchen day. All five products are prototypes, not on sale and not an approved launch range. All ten recipes listed below are **draft — kitchen testing required**. This filming plan does not approve them. Complete kitchen testing and recipe sign-off before filming instruction-ready versions; otherwise capture development footage only and retain the development notice.

A full day is the realistic option for all ten plated dishes. A half day covers a smaller first batch; do not rush cooking or pretend the remaining dishes were filmed. Store discovery v12 requires a separate real retailer visit after confirmation. A tasting c01 is optional and needs independent clearance and permission. Neither is necessary to make the kitchen day productive.

Never infer product ingredients, allergens, net content, heat level, shelf life or storage from product names, visible meal components or a prototype label. No dietary, nutrition, purity or similar claims. Any ownership, sourcing or manufacturing sentence must use “[relación de marca por confirmar]”. Never name a manufacturer or plant. Do not film confidential formulas or private business information.

## Crew and roles

- Producer/camera/editor: one person handles the phone, shot IDs, framing, sound check, permissions and clip log. Keep this person away from ready-to-eat food handling unless they wash hands and change roles properly.
- Cook/food lead: one competent person owns actual cooking, food handling, dish sign-off and safe holding/disposal decisions. They can appear as hands only with consent.
- Optional helper/team participant: resets props, checks continuity, holds reflector and records their own approved introduction. Confirm their actual role; do not automatically call them a founder or chef.
- Owner/approver: confirms prototype identities, visible labels, people, factual copy and permitted publication. Approval can happen remotely after the shoot; nothing publishes automatically.

Two people can manage this with a locked tripod. Use a third helper if filming all ten dishes. Do not ask the cook to operate the phone while handling food.

## Equipment

- [ ] Charged phone, sufficient free storage, clean lens, power bank and charging cable.
- [ ] Stable tripod with phone clamp; overhead mount only if secure and clear of food/heat.
- [ ] Window/natural light with diffusion as needed; white reflector board and clamps.
- [ ] Lav mic for v11, compatible adapter, headphones and a short sound test.
- [ ] Clean neutral table surface, plain plates, clean serving spoons and spare utensils.
- [ ] Appropriate kitchen sanitation supplies and food lead's normal monitoring equipment.
- [ ] Shot log on paper or a device outside the food work area; consent forms available.
- [ ] Existing storage/computer for two verified copies; no new paid service required.

## Before shoot day

- [ ] Confirm authorized access to the real kitchen and what backgrounds may be shown.
- [ ] Obtain actual samples of each named prototype; log identity and approval privately. Do not substitute generated pack shots or a different food in the container.
- [ ] Ask the responsible food lead for approved handling instructions and allergen information privately. If safe handling cannot be established, do not handle or serve the sample. Do not invent instructions or publish them as product facts.
- [ ] Have the cook review all ten draft recipes. Prepare a private shopping/prep list for the dishes they actually approve; this is meal preparation, not a product formulation list.
- [ ] Plan compatible meal components together, but maintain separation and safe handling. Prepare items in advance only under the cook's approved procedure.
- [ ] Prepare genuine packaging for identity shots; review every visible claim. Frame out or cover unapproved text without creating a fictitious retail label. Keep the original files private if they contain sensitive information.
- [ ] Print the shot matrix below. Mark MUST HAVE: actual application action and finished plate for each dish; real texture for each prototype; v11 introduction; v09/v10 actions.
- [ ] Prepare ES on-screen copy and v11's approved name/role sentence from document 06. English subtitles are made later, checked by a person.
- [ ] Remove private papers, screens, contact details, formulas, supplier information and unrelated branding from the frame.
- [ ] Confirm participant releases and whether anyone may taste. No public tasting without food lead clearance and necessary information for participants.
- [ ] Do not prepare a store backdrop, fake delivery or customer script. v12 and c01 are conditional.

Record each finished plate immediately; do not hold food for the camera at the expense of safe handling.

## Location and camera setup

- [ ] Put the filming table beside a window; use the reflector opposite. Avoid changing between window light and mixed overhead lighting within a sequence.
- [ ] Separate raw-food preparation, ready-to-eat plating and camera equipment. Keep stands and cables away from walking paths and cooking hazards.
- [ ] Shoot the real kitchen with operator permission, using tight framing if its background is private. Do not imply a domestic or test kitchen is a manufacturing facility.
- [ ] Record a short test and check focus, colour, sound and readable labels. Lock exposure/white balance if the phone allows it; avoid beautification and food-altering filters.
- [ ] Main footage: vertical 9:16, action in the centre with space for subtitles and interface overlays. Capture 5–8-second steady clips with a short pause before/after the action.
- [ ] Secondary footage: rotate to horizontal 16:9 for each final dish, each prototype and the introduction. Repeat safe actions only when practical; a wide still is sufficient when repeating would waste food or disrupt handling.
- [ ] Capture clean masters without baked-in subtitles/music; add approved graphics later. Never digitally alter product colour, consistency or contents.

## Permissions and release checklist

- [ ] Kitchen/property operator permission, including exact areas and permitted uses; location access is not permission to publish everything inside.
- [ ] Written likeness/voice releases for identifiable adults, specifying social use and approved edits; hands, uniforms or tattoos may also identify a person. Avoid minors and unconsented bystanders.
- [ ] Confirm name, role and approved introduction; founder/chef titles require verification.
- [ ] Owner approval for actual prototypes, packaging, labels and public product presentation.
- [ ] Participant permission for any tasting quote, with the exact final wording and context. Do not imply an independent customer endorsement from a team member.
- [ ] Retail permission and current availability evidence before any store footage; avoid visible customer identities, receipts or private systems.
- [ ] Rights to music, graphics and third-party assets. Do not rely on a platform music licence for exports to another platform without checking it.
- [ ] Keep signed releases and internal safety/approval records in restricted storage; the public repo receives neither personal data nor private business records.

## Food safety and hygiene during filming

The responsible cook controls safe preparation, cooking, holding, cleaning and disposal using their established procedures. A camera schedule is never a safety instruction. Wash hands, use clean utensils and work surfaces, separate raw and ready-to-eat foods, and prevent cross-contact. Keep phones and microphones off food-contact surfaces. Change utensils after tasting; never double-dip. Follow the cook's direction on safe temperature monitoring and exposure time without inventing product storage or shelf-life claims.

Do not offer prototypes to participants if allergen information and safe handling have not been established for that tasting. Food used repeatedly as a camera prop must not automatically be served. Stop filming when hygiene or safe handling cannot be maintained. Do not present hygiene footage as certification, inspection or quality-control evidence.

## Naming and storage

Use `YYYYMMDD_scene-shot_brief_product-or-dish_orientation_take.ext`, for example:

- `20261201_C01-02_v01_yuca_V_t01.mov`
- `20261201_C08-04_v07_bowl_H_t01.mov`
- `20261201_E01-01_v11_team_V_t02.mov`

`V` = vertical, `H` = horizontal. For shared footage use `shared` in the brief field and list all brief IDs in the clip log. Do not put private names or confidential batch information in public filenames.

Suggested private project structure: `2026-12/01_raw/`, `02_selects/`, `03_projects/`, `04_exports/`, `05_approvals/`, `06_releases_restricted/`. Preserve untouched originals; edit copies. Keep subtitles and editable project files with exports. Never upload raw private footage, releases or sensitive records into the public repository.

Clip log fields: filename, scene, brief IDs, actual sample/dish identity, orientation, usable audio, development/test status, permission status, confidential content flag and approver/date. Keep sensitive identity details in a separate restricted register. Make two copies on existing separate storage, open representative clips from both, and verify file counts/sizes before clearing the phone. Restrict access to people doing the work.
